## About

> Carson is always somewhere in the past or the future.

He studied software engineering, and it shaped the way he takes things apart — systems, structures, sequences. But somewhere along the way he became more interested in what happens after a system reaches someone's hands than in what it can do on paper.

Why a button makes people hesitate. Why one piece of feedback feels reassuring and another doesn't. Why a flow that is logically complete turns difficult the moment it meets real life. Technology is tidy on paper; it deforms when it enters a day.

He is now at KTH, in Interactive Media Technology. Less a change of geography than a place where his older questions — structure and feeling, technology and people — found room to keep growing.

He has grown careful with large statements. He trusts the local, the specific, the quieter moments that sit closer to a person.

## On Coding

Code, for him, is a way of organising complexity.

Python for data analysis, machine learning and experiments; JavaScript / TypeScript and React on the front end — page structure, component logic, the rhythm of an interaction.

Lately his attention has moved to the layer around the model rather than the model itself: how context is assembled, how tools and permissions are handed over, where a task should loop and where it ought to stop. That arrangement, more than raw capability, decides whether a system holds up in real work.

Since KTH he has also been working in interaction design — research, interviews, observation, prototypes. A second language for the same set of problems.

Code, here, tends to lead somewhere more concrete: interfaces, experience, products, and the small seams between people and the tools they use.
